Research

What I work on

I'm interested in how physical and biogeochemical processes shape microbial life in the ocean — in particular, how coastal upwelling systems create windows of opportunity for nitrogen-fixing microorganisms.

Diazotrophy & nitrogen fixation

Biological nitrogen fixation is a key process supplying "new" nitrogen to the ocean. I study the diazotroph community (including unicellular cyanobacteria such as UCYN-A) in coastal waters, and the environmental conditions — temperature, nutrient ratios, stratification — that trigger or suppress their activity.

Coastal upwelling biogeochemistry

The Rías Baixas (NW Spain) are a highly productive upwelling system. I look at how wind-driven upwelling and relaxation cycles reshape nutrient stoichiometry (N:P ratios) and create transient ecological niches for nitrogen fixers, extending the productive season beyond what classical nutrient budgets would predict.

Time series, data quality & numerical analysis

Much of this work relies on high-resolution oceanographic time series and shipboard/ incubation experiments. I build data-processing and analysis pipelines (MATLAB/Python) to quality-control, visualize and model these datasets — see the code on GitHub.

Group

This work is carried out within the Biological Oceanography Group at CIM-UVIGO (Centro de Investigación Mariña, Universidade de Vigo), led by Beatriz Mouriño-Carballido, in collaboration with IIM-CSIC, the University of Southampton, Linnaeus University and other partners.
